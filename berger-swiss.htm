Pairing System

  • TPNs are assigned in initial descending rating order and never change. Equal ratings retain their input order.
  • After all exchanges, each Berger group is independently randomized. The bottom Swiss group is placed in rating order and plays Swiss rounds from Round 1.
  • In a retained Berger group, a withdrawn player remains on the fixed schedule. An active opponent receives a one-point Berger bye; the withdrawn player receives zero. If both scheduled players have withdrawn, both receive zero. These automatic results add no played color, encounter, or direct WNT.
  • Swiss pairing order is descending score, descending WNT, then ascending TPN. The search avoids rematches where possible, limits same-color runs, considers eligible bye recipients from the bottom of the standings, and then considers color balance and score differences.
  • Swiss colors are selected by comparing both assignments. Preference is given to shorter same-color runs, followed by color balance and alternation. If these are equal, White goes to the player with fewer previous White games, or to the higher TPN if equal.
  • Swiss pairing byes score one point. Later Swiss bye recipients must have the fewest previous pairing byes among active players in the Swiss field. Berger byes count toward that eligibility. Scheduled Berger byes are not subject to Swiss bye selection.
  • A withdrawal takes effect from the next unpaired round. The current published round is not changed. Enter its result normally, or choose an explicit forfeit if it was not played.

About the Berger-Swiss System

Berger-Swiss combines fixed round-robin groups with Swiss pairings. Players are initially grouped by rating, with exchanges between adjacent groups to broaden opposition.

Group construction and interchange

Interchange positions refer to the original rating order within each group. After all exchanges, each Berger group is independently shuffled. The bottom Swiss group remains in rating order. TPNs do not change.

Withdrawal setting

The settings-page checkbox “Convert affected Berger groups to Swiss after two withdrawals in a group” is checked by default. Its value is saved with the tournament and cannot be changed after the tournament starts.

When unchecked, every Berger group retains its fixed schedule for the remainder of the Berger phase. An existing bottom Swiss field continues independently.

When checked, two or more withdrawals from the same Berger group normally cause that group and all subsequent Berger groups to join the existing Swiss field. A single withdrawal in a Berger group does not trigger conversion; the group retains its schedule with scheduled byes. Earlier Berger groups retain their schedules. The exception is an isolated last Berger group: if there is no bottom Swiss field and only the last Berger group is affected, that group retains its schedule. A later second withdrawal from an earlier Berger group can still cause conversion.

A withdrawal takes effect from the next unpaired round. The current published round and its entered results remain unchanged. No result is automatically assigned to that current round.

In every retained Berger group, the withdrawn player’s name remains in the scheduled pairings. If the opponent is active, the opponent receives a one-point Berger bye and the withdrawn player receives zero. If both scheduled players have withdrawn, both receive zero. These automatic results cannot be edited.

A Berger bye adds no played color, encounter, or direct WNT. It counts as a pairing bye for later Swiss bye eligibility. A retained group may produce multiple Berger byes in one round, or multiple Berger byes for the same player over different rounds.

If a group converts to Swiss, its unplayed Berger schedule is canceled; no results are invented for those canceled games. Withdrawn players remain in the standings, but are not paired in Swiss rounds.

If fewer than two active players remain, the tournament normally finishes. If exactly one active player remains in a retained Berger group during the Berger phase, its scheduled rounds can continue until the combined Swiss phase or tournament completion.

Swiss pairing

Players are ordered by descending score, descending WNT, and ascending TPN. The highest remaining player is paired first. Candidate opponents are considered by previous meetings, score difference, color penalties, and standings order, subject to a complete pairing of the remaining field.

The search first excludes rematches and seeks a complete pairing without a third consecutive game of the same color. At each color-run limit, eligible bye recipients are considered from the bottom of the standings. The first recipient permitting a complete pairing is selected before score gaps and total color balance are optimized.

For the selected recipient, the search first tries to keep each player’s White and Black totals within two games. Maximum score differences of zero, half a point, one point, and unrestricted are tried in that order. If necessary, total-balance restrictions are relaxed. Longer color runs are permitted only when necessary.

Rematches are permitted only after every no-rematch possibility has failed for every eligible bye recipient. The search prefers fewer previous meetings, but does not guarantee the minimum total number of rematches.

Color allocation

Berger colors follow the fixed Berger table. For Swiss pairings, both color assignments are compared. Preference is given first to shorter same-color runs, then to color balance and alternation. If equal, White goes to the player with fewer previous White games, or to the higher TPN if equal.

Byes and forfeits add no played color and do not interrupt a played color streak. Unresolved postponed Berger games temporarily retain their scheduled colors.

Postponed games and forfeits

Select ‘p’ for a postponed Berger game. Until resolved, both players receive zero points and no direct WNT, but the scheduled colors and encounter remain allocated. Submitted Berger results remain editable while their group retains its Berger schedule.

For an unplayed game in the current published round, use an explicit forfeit result. Forfeits award the indicated points but add no played colors, encounter, or direct WNT. A forfeit win is not treated as a pairing bye by this custom system.

WNT

WNT = 100 × Σ [(Pᵢ / N) × (rᵢ + Pᵢ / (2N))] Pᵢ = opponent i's current tournament score rᵢ = result in the played game: 1, ½, or 0 N = number of submitted rounds The sum is over played games. Byes, forfeits, automatic withdrawal results, and unresolved postponed games are excluded.

WNT is recalculated from all submitted rounds. Repeated played encounters contribute separately. Results entered in an unsubmitted round do not yet affect scores or tie-breaks.

Field sizes and byes

In an odd initial Swiss field, the lowest-rated player receives the first bye. Later Swiss bye recipients must have the fewest previous pairing byes among active players in that field. Eligible players are considered from the bottom of the standings, subject to a complete pairing.

The setup round limit is based on the original field size. It is a program policy, not a guarantee that all planned rounds remain possible after withdrawals.

Running tournaments in parallel

You can run separate tournaments simultaneously in different browsers (for example, Chrome and Edge). Each browser maintains its own autosave, so a tournament should be resumed in the browser where it was started. Avoid running separate tournaments in multiple tabs or windows within the same browser profile, as their autosaves may overwrite one another.

Browser storage saves automatically when available. For added safety, save JSON backups regularly. No data is sent to a server and tabs do not synchronize.

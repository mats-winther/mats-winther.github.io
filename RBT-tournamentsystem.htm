About Rating-Budget Tournament System

The RBT phase creates its complete schedule before the first round. Its pairings do not depend on results. Optionally, a second Swiss phase pairs active players by their accumulated results. Set Swiss Rounds to zero to use the original RBT-only system.

Each player has a personal group of nearby players in rating order, containing approximately one-third of the field and excluding the player themselves. The group’s average rating multiplied by the number of scheduled RBT games gives the player’s rating budget. An average of 1800 over nine games gives a budget of 16,200. Some opponents may be stronger and others weaker; the aim is to bring their combined ratings close to that target.

Matching these budgets is the main RBT priority. The system also prefers at least half of each player’s RBT games to be against opponents from their personal group, but this is not guaranteed.

The fixed RBT schedule has no repeated scheduled opponents. Scheduled White/Black totals are balanced as evenly as possible, and the search tries to avoid long same-colour runs. In an odd original field, one player rests each RBT round and no player has more than one scheduled RBT rest. A rest adds no opponent rating and reduces the budget by one personal target.

Personal rating groups

TPN 1 is assigned to the highest-rated player. Equal ratings retain roster order. Permanent TPNs never change.

N = number of players R = number of scheduled RBT rounds x = the player’s one-based TPN h = ceil(N / 6) g = 2 × h FirstTPN[x] = max(1, min(x − h, N − g)) LastTPN[x] = FirstTPN[x] + g PersonalGroup[x] = all TPNs from FirstTPN[x] through LastTPN[x], excluding x

Group size is N / 3 rounded upwards to an even integer. Ideally there are h opponents above the player and h below. Near either end, unavailable places are transferred to the other side. Every personal group contains exactly g eligible opponents. With 60 players, h = 10 and g = 20. TPN 1’s group contains TPNs 2–21; TPN 30’s group contains 20–29 and 31–40; TPN 60’s group contains 40–59. With 50 players, g = 18.

Individual rating targets and budgets

PersonalTarget[x] = sum of ratings in PersonalGroup[x] / g ScheduledRBTGames[x] = R − ScheduledRBTRests[x] PersonalBudget[x] = ScheduledRBTGames[x] × PersonalTarget[x] Primary objective: Minimize Σ over players |AssignedRBTOpponentRatingSum[x] − PersonalBudget[x]|

The player’s own rating is excluded from the player’s group mean. Budgets use scheduled RBT games, not recorded results or games actually played. Swiss games are excluded from budget diagnostics. Overlapping group means need not preserve the field’s total rating, so zero total budget error is not always possible. For an odd field, the aggregate lower bound applies to the schedule’s current rest allocation.

Secondary personal-group preference

RequestedGroupOpponents[x] = ceil(ScheduledRBTGames[x] / 2) GroupShortfall[x] = max(0, RequestedGroupOpponents[x] − GroupOpponents[x]) Optimization priorities: 1. Total absolute personal-budget error 2. Total personal-group encounter shortfall 3. Total TPN distance

There is no additional +1 for an even number of games. A scheduled rest is not a game. Budget accuracy takes precedence. Group shortfalls can remain because of budget priorities, structural limitations or bounded search. Without rematches, no player can face more than g distinct personal-group opponents. Membership is directional and is counted separately for each player in a game.

How RBT scheduling works

Several valid round-robin-derived schedules are generated. Round selection and legal within-round exchanges improve the rating-budget objective without repeated opponents or multiple appearances in a round.

Balanced Euler orientation assigns colours. Each player receives equal scheduled White/Black totals for an even number of RBT games, or a difference of one for an odd number. Directed-cycle reversals and round-order exchanges reduce streaks without changing opponents or colour totals.

This is a randomized, bounded heuristic search, not a guarantee of global optimality. Remaining errors, group shortfalls and long colour streaks are reported. Regenerating before recording results may improve the schedule, but improvement is not guaranteed.

RBT rests

A player without a rest has a budget of R × PersonalTarget. A player with one rest has a budget of (R − 1) × PersonalTarget. The requested group count also uses scheduled games. Moving a rest recalculates both values. Rest points are configurable and default to one. They are credited when all games in that RBT round have results.

Converting an RBT tournament to Swiss

If players have withdrawn, resulting in too many bye rounds, the administrator may choose to switch to the Swiss system. Before the Swiss phase begins, select a completed RBT round and choose Convert to Swiss. Every game through the selected round must have a result. Later RBT rounds must have no manually entered results; automatic withdrawal forfeits are permitted.

The completed RBT rounds are retained and locked. Later fixed RBT pairings and their automatic forfeits are discarded. The remaining planned rounds become Swiss rounds, preserving the original total number of planned rounds. The first Swiss round is generated immediately.

Withdrawal records and their effective rounds are retained. A player whose withdrawal takes effect later remains eligible until that round. Withdrawals cannot be reversed in the Swiss interface. At least two players must be active in the first Swiss round.

After conversion, standings use the hybrid calculation. WNT is recalculated from completed rounds: direct contributions from forfeits and byes are excluded, and the denominator is the number of included completed rounds. Consequently, WNT and standings may change from the RBT-only view. Swiss pairing uses score, WNT and TPN. Future automatic forfeits and any points provisionally credited because of them are discarded.

Rating-budget diagnostics are inapplicable after conversion. Retained RBT rounds are not required to have balanced colour totals because they form only part of the original schedule. Save a JSON backup before converting.

Optional Swiss phase

All RBT games must have played or explicit forfeit results before the first Swiss round is generated. RBT results then lock. Swiss rounds are paired and submitted sequentially. Results in an unsubmitted Swiss round are saved but do not yet affect scores, tie-breaks or pairing histories. A submitted Swiss round is locked.

RBT rounds may be played in any order before Swiss begins. For Swiss colour histories, the program uses the scheduled chronological round order, not the order in which games or results were entered. Colour-streak recommendations therefore refer to that order.

Swiss pairing order is descending score, descending WNT and ascending TPN, regardless of the selected display tie-break. The highest remaining player is paired first. Candidate opponents are considered by score difference, colour penalties and standings order, and each selection must allow a complete pairing.

The search exhausts no-rematch possibilities before permitting rematches. It first seeks pairings without a third consecutive identical played colour. At each colour-run limit, eligible bye recipients are tried from the bottom of the Swiss standings. The first feasible recipient is fixed before score differences and total colour balance are optimized.

For that recipient, the search prefers White/Black differences within two. Maximum score differences of zero, half a point, one point and unrestricted differences are tried in that order. If needed, total-balance restrictions are relaxed and those differences are tried again. The colour-run limit increases only when no eligible recipient is feasible at the current limit.

Rematches are allowed only if no complete pairing without rematches exists for any eligible recipient, even after colour and score-gap restrictions are relaxed. In that fallback, previously unplayed opponents are preferred, then fewer previous meetings, score difference, colour penalties and standings order. This top-down preference does not guarantee the minimum total number of rematches. Rematches are marked.

Swiss colours and byes

Both colour assignments are compared. Priority is given to limiting identical-colour runs, reducing streak violations, maintaining total colour balance and alternating. If equal, White goes to the player with fewer previous White games, or the higher TPN if equal. Played RBT colours carry forward. Rests and forfeits add no played colour and do not interrupt a played-colour streak.

Completed RBT rests count toward Swiss bye eligibility, even if their configured score is zero. Eligible active players have the fewest previous rests/byes. A repeat bye is possible only when every active player has already received at least one. Swiss byes use the configured rest-point value and contribute no direct WNT. A forfeit win is not counted as a pairing bye.

Played RBT encounters carry forward. A forfeited pairing is not treated as a played encounter and may be scheduled again. Withdrawn players retain previous scores and results. Fewer than two active players ends the Swiss continuation. Planned RBT plus Swiss rounds are limited to N − 1 for an even original field or N for an odd field. This is a program policy, not a guarantee of rematch-free continuation.

Standings and tie-breaks

With Swiss Rounds = 0, the original RBT scoring and tie-break policies are retained. Recorded individual results affect standings immediately, WNT includes forfeits, and its denominator is the scheduled RBT round count.

RBT-only WNT = 100 × Σ [(Pᵢ / R) × (rᵢ + Pᵢ / (2R))] R = scheduled RBT rounds Pᵢ = opponent’s recorded tournament score rᵢ = awarded result, including forfeits

In an RBT–Swiss tournament, standings include only complete RBT rounds and submitted Swiss rounds. During the RBT phase, complete rounds remain editable until Swiss begins. Hybrid WNT excludes direct contributions from forfeits and byes. Opponents’ tournament scores still include all awarded points.

Hybrid WNT = 100 × Σ [(Pᵢ / K) × (rᵢ + Pᵢ / (2K))] K = included complete RBT rounds + submitted Swiss rounds Pᵢ = opponent’s current included tournament score rᵢ = result of a played game: 1, ½ or 0 The sum includes played games only. Repeated played encounters contribute separately.

Progressive adds cumulative scores in scheduled round order. In RBT-only mode it includes rounds with recorded results. In hybrid mode it includes complete RBT and submitted Swiss rounds. Buchholz Cut-1 adds included opponents’ scores and removes the lowest entry; credited rests/byes contribute zero-point opponents. Forfeited pairings remain included in this application’s Buchholz. These are custom treatments, not FIDE unplayed-game adjustments.

W/D/L include forfeits, while Pld counts played games. Players still tied after the selected tie-break share a position; TPN determines display order. Swiss pairing itself always uses WNT. Displaying the final/current view does not submit a Swiss round, unlock results or terminate an unfinished Swiss phase.

Withdrawals and saving

Before Swiss starts, RBT withdrawal/reinstatement retains the fixed schedule. Blank affected RBT games receive automatic forfeits; manual results remain unchanged. Reinstatement removes automatic forfeits that no longer apply. A hybrid withdrawal can also be set to begin at the first Swiss round.

After Swiss begins, withdrawals are announced in the latest unpublished-result round and take effect from the next unpaired round. Current pairings and results do not change, and no forfeit is automatically assigned in Swiss. Swiss withdrawals cannot be reversed in this interface. Enter an explicit forfeit if a current scheduled game was not played.

Running tournaments in parallel

You can run separate tournaments simultaneously in different browsers (for example, Chrome and Edge). Each browser maintains its own autosave, so a tournament should be resumed in the browser where it was started. Avoid running separate tournaments in multiple tabs or windows within the same browser profile, as their autosaves may overwrite one another.

Browser storage saves automatically when available. For added safety, save JSON backups regularly. No data is sent to a server and tabs do not synchronize.

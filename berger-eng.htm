About the Berger Tournament Manager

This standalone application maintains four separate tournament slots. Each slot has its own roster, schedule, results, selected round, tie-break setting, and withdrawal records.

  • Pairing numbers follow the order of names in the roster.
  • For an odd roster, the unused highest pairing number is a bye. A scheduled rest round awards no points.
  • In a double round-robin, the last two rounds of the first cycle are transposed. The second cycle uses the base schedule with colours reversed.
  • Sonneborn-Berger uses opponents' scores multiplied by points earned against them. Round-robin forfeits are included.
  • Direct Encounter averages repeated encounters with each opponent. It is reapplied to smaller tied groups when appropriate.
  • Progressive Score adds the player's cumulative score after each included round.
  • The selected tie-break is the only tie-break applied. Remaining ties share a position. Pairing number controls display order within an unresolved tie, not sporting rank.
  • Current standings include results through the selected round. Final standings include every round.

Conversion to Swiss

If players have withdrawn from the tournament, the tournament director may choose to switch to Swiss pairing in order to avoid an excessive number of byes. The planned number of rounds is retained, which means that repeat pairings may occur towards the end of the tournament.

Select a Berger round and choose Convert to Swiss. Every real game through that round must have a result. Later Berger rounds must not contain manually entered results or forfeits. Later automatic withdrawal forfeits and their points are discarded with the future Berger schedule.

The completed Berger rounds are retained and locked. Withdrawal dates are retained, including withdrawals effective in a future round. The remaining originally planned rounds become Swiss rounds, paired one at a time. If fewer than two active players remain before a subsequent Swiss round, the tournament ends early.

Swiss pairings and standings use score, Progressive Score, Sonneborn-Berger, and pairing number, in that order. Pairing number controls the display and pairing order of players still tied; those players share their sporting position.

Progressive Score adds the cumulative score after each counted round. Sonneborn-Berger includes the direct contributions of forfeits. A Swiss bye scores one point and has no opponent contribution to Sonneborn-Berger. A scheduled Berger rest retains its original zero-point value and does not count as a previous Swiss bye.

Played Berger colours and encounters carry forward. Forfeits and byes add no played colour or encounter and do not interrupt a played-colour streak. Swiss pairing avoids rematches whenever a complete pairing without rematches is possible; otherwise it permits rematches as a last resort.

Results entered in an unsubmitted Swiss round are saved but do not affect standings or the next pairing. Submitting the round locks its results. Swiss withdrawals take effect from the next unpaired round. Previously recorded withdrawal dates remain in effect.

Withdrawal handling is an administrative convenience: existing results remain unchanged, while unrecorded games from the specified round receive forfeits. The application does not automatically annul earlier results.

Automatic saving uses this browser's local storage when available. Download JSON backups regularly. This application does not synchronize different devices or browser tabs.

Withdraw or reinstate a player

Recorded manual results are preserved. Unrecorded games from the effective round onward become forfeit losses for the withdrawn player. If both players are withdrawn, the result is a double forfeit.

Reinstatement removes automatically assigned forfeits that no longer apply. Manually entered results are not removed. No earlier results are automatically annulled.
